I am writing to request partnership and Emergency API access for UNITED, a voice-triggered emergency alert web app I built for moments when someone cannot reach their phone or speak — domestic violence situations, medical emergencies, or any crisis where calling 911 directly is not possible.

Exactly what UNITED does, step by step:

1. Always-on voice trigger. On page load the app begins listening (Web Speech API). Saying "Stay Calm" triggers the emergency flow hands-free. A large on-screen EMERGENCY button works as a tap fallback, and double-tapping it stops recording and voice.

2. On trigger, three things happen at once:
   - Discreet audio recording begins. The microphone only activates at trigger — never before.
   - High-accuracy GPS location is captured. Location warmup starts at page load so the fix is ready when it matters.
   - A calm voice loop plays reassurance messages: "Help is coming. You are not alone." / "Breathe slowly. Stay with me." / "You are doing great. Help is on the way."

3. Confirmation before dispatch. The user confirms SEND on screen, and a separate "Confirm SEND to EMS" acknowledgment is required before anything goes to live dispatch.

4. Dispatch. The app transmits an alert package: GPS coordinates, timestamp, a universal incident code, and a caller note ("Voice-triggered alert. Caller may be unable to speak."), plus the audio recording. In LIVE mode this goes to EMS through your Emergency API. The app currently runs in TEST mode to a test relay.

5. Stand-down. When responders arrive, the spoken phrase "help has arrived" deactivates the alert.
