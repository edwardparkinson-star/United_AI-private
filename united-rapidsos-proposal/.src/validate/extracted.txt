EXECUTIVE PROPOSAL

A guided pilot is the right next step - not a production
launch
UNITED gives a person two deliberate ways to begin an emergency
workflow: speak “Stay Calm” or tap EMERGENCY. The prototype then
gathers location and incident context; RapidSOS can help determine
the safe, supported route from that trigger to professional verification
and public safety.

The proposed decision
Assign a RapidSOS partnership and technical contact for a discovery session. The output should be a
written integration path, sandbox plan, and joint go/no-go criteria for a controlled pilot.

Why the fit is credible
RapidSOS publicly describes a path for apps, wearables, and personal emergency response systems to
connect users to a RapidSOS Safety Agent or 911 and to provide critical data through a single API.[1] UNITED
is not claiming that this connection already exists. It is presenting a working interaction prototype and
requesting the partnership needed to build the dispatch layer correctly.

Recommended event lifecycle
1. Enroll and consent: verify the user/device, request permissions in context, and record acceptance of
emergency-service terms.
2. Create an intent: “Stay Calm” or EMERGENCY opens an incident locally; it does not by itself claim dispatch
success.
3. Confirm and enrich: collect a fresh location fix, accuracy, device timestamp, callback identity if permitted,
and optional user-entered context.
4. Submit server-side: the backend authenticates to the approved RapidSOS service, assigns an
idempotency key, and records the response.
5. Show truthful status: distinguish submitted, accepted, connected to a Safety Agent, delivered to public
safety, failed, and canceled.
6. Close safely: process cancellation/stand-down according to RapidSOS rules and retain only the minimum
audit data required.

S A F E T Y, P R I VA C Y, A N D O P E R AT I O N S

Trust depends on preventing false confidence, false
alarms, and exposed data
Product safety controls

Data protection controls

Keep TEST and production environments visibly
and technically separate.

Keep RapidSOS credentials and signing material
on the backend.

Require an intentional confirmation or the
RapidSOS-approved verification path.

Encrypt transport and any retained incident data.

Offer a visible cancel action and an accessible nonvoice fallback.
Never promise response until an authoritative
status supports that message.
Fail safely when microphone, location, network, or
background execution is unavailable.
Rate-limit duplicate triggers without suppressing a
genuine new event.

Collect only fields required for response and userapproved context.
Set short, explicit retention periods for recordings
and location history.
Use role-based access, immutable incident logs,
and security monitoring.
Document deletion, incident response, and userdata request procedures.

Audio requires a specific policy decision
The prototype records ambient audio after activation. An earlier RapidSOS partner example describes
audio being recorded during a 911 call,[3] but that does not prove that UNITED can upload audio through
the same route. The team should not send or retain audio until RapidSOS confirms the supported
workflow and legal, consent, evidentiary, and retention requirements are reviewed.

REFERENCE NOTES

Official RapidSOS material supports the fit - not a
claim of approval
The proposal uses RapidSOS’s own public descriptions to frame the
partnership opportunity. The exact product, workflow, schema, commercial
terms, and approval path remain questions for RapidSOS.
What the public material establishes
RapidSOS markets safety capabilities to technology companies, including apps, wearables, and
personal emergency response systems.
It describes direct links to RapidSOS Safety Agents or 911 and API-based delivery of critical
emergency data.
Its emergency API material discusses sharing connected-device data with Emergency Communication
Centers.
An e-BodyGuard partnership offers a relevant precedent for voice or button activation, user-provided
safety data, and ambient audio associated with a 911 call.
