UNITED / CybersymanticsProposal 01
Executive proposal

A guided pilot is the right next step - not a production launch

UNITED gives a person two deliberate ways to begin an emergency workflow: speak “Stay Calm” or tap EMERGENCY. The prototype then gathers location and incident context; RapidSOS can help determine the safe, supported route from that trigger to professional verification and public safety.

What exists now

  • A browser-based prototype in TEST mode.
  • Voice phrase and large-button activation.
  • GPS warm-up, fresh location capture, and accuracy metadata.
  • Post-trigger audio recording and spoken reassurance.
  • Separate test and intended live-dispatch code paths.

What UNITED is asking RapidSOS for

  1. Confirm the appropriate product path: safety-agent-assisted response, direct 911 data delivery, or a combined workflow.
  2. Provide technical discovery and sandbox access.
  3. Define the event schema, verification rules, testing process, and production-readiness requirements.
  4. Co-design a limited pilot before any public launch.

The proposed decision

Assign a RapidSOS partnership and technical contact for a discovery session. The output should be a written integration path, sandbox plan, and joint go/no-go criteria for a controlled pilot.

Why the fit is credible

RapidSOS publicly describes a path for apps, wearables, and personal emergency response systems to connect users to a RapidSOS Safety Agent or 911 and to provide critical data through a single API.[1] UNITED is not claiming that this connection already exists. It is presenting a working interaction prototype and requesting the partnership needed to build the dispatch layer correctly.

User need and safety experience

The product is designed for moments when a normal call is difficult

UNITED is intended as a supplemental safety tool for users who may be injured, panicked, unable to reach the screen quickly, or unable to sustain a normal voice call. It does not replace calling or texting 911 when those options are available.

The experience to preserve

  • Low-friction activation: voice when hands are unavailable; a large visual control when voice is unavailable.
  • Clear state: the interface must show TEST, armed, confirming, sending, accepted, failed, and canceled states.
  • Calm guidance: short reassurance should never imply that help is coming until delivery and response status are actually known.
  • User control: cancellation and correction must be obvious and safe.

A safety issue to test early

“Stay Calm” is ordinary conversational language. It may be easier to remember than a coded phrase, but it could also trigger accidentally. The pilot should measure false activations and compare safeguards such as a two-stage confirmation, a configurable phrase, device state checks, and a short cancel window.

Non-negotiable product truth

Until RapidSOS confirms the workflow and controlled testing succeeds, UNITED must remain in TEST mode and must not display “help is on the way” merely because a network request was attempted.

Initial use cases for discovery

Medical distress

A user can speak but cannot navigate a call.

Personal safety

A discreet trigger may be safer than a visible call.

Mobility constraint

The device is nearby but not reachable by hand.

Recommended event lifecycle

  1. Enroll and consent: verify the user/device, request permissions in context, and record acceptance of emergency-service terms.
  2. Create an intent: “Stay Calm” or EMERGENCY opens an incident locally; it does not by itself claim dispatch success.
  3. Confirm and enrich: collect a fresh location fix, accuracy, device timestamp, callback identity if permitted, and optional user-entered context.
  4. Submit server-side: the backend authenticates to the approved RapidSOS service, assigns an idempotency key, and records the response.
  5. Show truthful status: distinguish submitted, accepted, connected to a Safety Agent, delivered to public safety, failed, and canceled.
  6. Close safely: process cancellation/stand-down according to RapidSOS rules and retain only the minimum audit data required.

Proposed minimum incident data

  • Opaque incident and device IDs
  • Trigger type and confirmation state
  • Timestamp with time zone
  • Latitude, longitude, accuracy, and fix age
  • Requested service and short situation note
  • Callback channel only with consent

RapidSOS must define

  • Approved API, SDK, or monitoring workflow
  • Required identity and verification fields
  • Schema, authentication, rate limits, and acknowledgments
  • Audio/media support and retention rules
  • Cancellation, duplicate, and stale-event handling
  • Geographic coverage and production certification

Safety, privacy, and operations

Trust depends on preventing false confidence, false alarms, and exposed data

Product safety controls

  • Keep TEST and production environments visibly and technically separate.
  • Require an intentional confirmation or the RapidSOS-approved verification path.
  • Offer a visible cancel action and an accessible non-voice fallback.
  • Never promise response until an authoritative status supports that message.
  • Fail safely when microphone, location, network, or background execution is unavailable.
  • Rate-limit duplicate triggers without suppressing a genuine new event.

Data protection controls

  • Keep RapidSOS credentials and signing material on the backend.
  • Encrypt transport and any retained incident data.
  • Collect only fields required for response and user-approved context.
  • Set short, explicit retention periods for recordings and location history.
  • Use role-based access, immutable incident logs, and security monitoring.
  • Document deletion, incident response, and user-data request procedures.

Audio requires a specific policy decision

The prototype records ambient audio after activation. An earlier RapidSOS partner example describes audio being recorded during a 911 call,[3] but that does not prove that UNITED can upload audio through the same route. The team should not send or retain audio until RapidSOS confirms the supported workflow and legal, consent, evidentiary, and retention requirements are reviewed.

Pilot plan

Each stage should end with evidence, not optimism

Stage 1

Technical discovery

Choose the supported RapidSOS product path and document the end-to-end sequence, required data, authentication, acknowledgments, cancellation behavior, coverage, and commercial prerequisites.

Exit: signed-off integration brief and named owners.

Stage 2

Sandbox integration

Build the backend, replace browser-held secrets, implement schema validation and idempotency, and exercise success and failure states with synthetic incidents only.

Exit: repeatable automated tests and reviewed logs.

Stage 3

Controlled end-to-end tests

Run planned tests with RapidSOS oversight. Validate routing, acknowledgments, cancellation, stale location, network loss, duplicate triggers, and device/browser variability.

Exit: jointly accepted test report and resolved critical defects.

Stage 4

Limited field pilot

Invite a small, jointly agreed cohort under clear support and monitoring procedures. Keep rollout reversible and publish only verified capabilities.

Exit: production-readiness decision based on measured safety and reliability.

Discussion agenda and decision

The first meeting should resolve ten concrete questions

1. Workflow: Should UNITED connect first to a RapidSOS Safety Agent, directly to 911 data delivery, or to another supported service?

2. Trigger verification: What human or technical verification is required before an incident reaches public safety?

3. Interface: Which API or SDK is appropriate for a mobile web prototype and a future native application?

4. Identity: Which user, device, callback, and consent fields are mandatory?

5. Event contract: What schema, status model, idempotency behavior, and acknowledgment semantics apply?

6. Media: Can ambient audio be shared, and if so, under what format, consent, and retention rules?

7. Coverage: Where is the proposed workflow supported, and what fallback is required outside coverage?

8. Testing: What sandbox, test numbers, scripted scenarios, and public-safety coordination are available?

9. Assurance: What security, privacy, insurance, legal, and certification reviews are prerequisites?

10. Commercial path: What pricing, minimums, support model, and launch commitments apply to an early-stage product?

Schedule a technical and partnership discovery call with the RapidSOS team. UNITED will provide a live TEST-mode demonstration and source walkthrough. RapidSOS would identify the supported product path and the requirements for sandbox access.
