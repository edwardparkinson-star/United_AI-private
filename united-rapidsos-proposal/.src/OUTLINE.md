Section: The safety gap UNITED targets
Claim: A deliberate voice or tap trigger can give users a low-friction way to begin an emergency workflow when calling may be difficult, while retaining confirmation and a visible fallback.
Source: Local UNITED code; product rationale stated by Edward Parkinson.

Section: The prototype already proves the interaction model
Claim: The browser prototype implements trigger detection, GPS warm-up and capture, audio recording, test/live separation, and a dispatch payload placeholder, but it does not yet provide a production-grade RapidSOS integration.
Source: united-index.html and united-voice-gps.js.

Section: RapidSOS is the appropriate integration partner
Claim: RapidSOS publicly positions its product for apps, wearables, and personal emergency response systems, with API-based delivery of critical data to safety agents or 911.
Source: RapidSOS official tech-company and apps/wearables pages.

Section: A secure server-side architecture is required
Claim: Production should move credentials, policy enforcement, event creation, retry logic, audit records, and media handling off the browser and into an authenticated backend shaped by RapidSOS requirements.
Source: Code review; standard security architecture; RapidSOS onboarding dependency explicitly marked in local code.

Section: A staged pilot can validate safety before launch
Claim: Sandbox contract definition, controlled end-to-end testing, limited field trials, and production readiness review reduce false activation and delivery risk.
Source: Proposal recommendation grounded in prototype gaps.
