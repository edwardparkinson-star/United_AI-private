Concept and pilot proposal

Responder Proximity System

A secure, in-vehicle view that shows an assigned first responder how close they are to the person who needs help - without exposing responder locations to the public.

Designed as a CAD/AVL and RapidSOS integration for emergency communications centers, agencies, and field responders.

UNITED RESPONDER PROXIMITY SYSTEMEXECUTIVE BRIEF
The decision

Build the view inside the dispatch chain

The concept is feasible, but it should not be a public “radar.” It should be an assignment-scoped proximity layer connected to the agency's computer-aided dispatch (CAD) and automatic vehicle location (AVL) systems.

01

Victim location

UNITED sends the emergency location, accuracy, timestamp, and incident context into an approved emergency-data pathway.

02

Responder location

The agency's AVL feed supplies the assigned vehicle's trusted GPS position. UNITED never publishes fleet locations.

03

Proximity view

The vehicle mobile data terminal (MDT) receives road distance, estimated time of arrival (ETA), direction, and data-freshness warnings.

Recommended product definition: “UNITED Responder Proximity” is a field-responder interface and integration service. It supplements existing CAD navigation; it does not dispatch units, replace 9-1-1, or expose responders on a consumer map.

Why this architecture fits public safety

  • RapidSOS describes integrations that place emergency data inside existing CAD, call-handling, mapping, and field-responder workflows.
  • National 911 guidance defines Next Generation 9-1-1 (NG911) as a secure, open-standards system that delivers emergency data to appropriate agencies and interoperates with first-responder networks.
  • APCO's CAD procurement guidance expects unit GPS, calls, and officer locations to be plotted together so dispatchers can assign the closest appropriate unit.

The first ask

Request a RapidSOS technical discovery session, data-partner pathway, sandbox access, and introductions to one participating CAD/field-responder integration partner.

The first pilot

One agency, one dispatch center, one CAD vendor, and a small vehicle set in shadow mode before live operational use.

UNITED RESPONDER PROXIMITY SYSTEMOPERATIONAL CONCEPT
What happens during an alert

CAD remains the source of truth

UNITED contributes incident-side data. The emergency communications center validates and dispatches. The assigned vehicle sees only the information needed to reach the incident safely.

1. Person initiates UNITED. The consumer experience may recognize the phrase “Stay Calm” or a manual emergency action, but confirmation and local emergency procedures govern transmission.
2. UNITED forms an incident package. It includes a unique incident ID, location, horizontal accuracy, timestamp, movement state, callback/contact channel, and the minimum relevant context.
3. Approved intake reaches the emergency communications center. RapidSOS Digital Alerts or another approved channel places the event into an operator workflow; local policy determines acceptance and handling.
4. CAD creates or links the call. The dispatcher validates the incident, assigns the appropriate unit, and remains authoritative for status, priority, cancellation, and reassignment.
5. The assigned vehicle receives proximity guidance. A CAD extension or MDT app combines the incident position with the vehicle's AVL position and a routing engine.
6. The view closes automatically. Access ends when the assignment is cancelled, transferred, cleared, or expires under agency policy.

Information shown to an assigned crew

  • Incident pin: coordinates plus accuracy radius
  • Road distance: route-based, not straight-line only
  • ETA: identified as an estimate
  • Direction: next turn or bearing
  • Freshness: age of victim and AVL fixes
  • Movement: stationary, moving, or unknown
  • Assignment: dispatched, en route, arrived, cleared
  • Hazards: only dispatcher-approved notes
Operational boundary: The vehicle view never self-dispatches, selects a unit, promises an arrival time to the victim, or bypasses the dispatcher. If any location is stale or uncertain, it says so plainly and falls back to the CAD location.

Interoperability posture

  • Map local CAD fields to a neutral internal model. Support vendor adapters rather than hard-coding one CAD.
  • Align incident exchange with public-safety standards. APCO/NENA's Emergency Incident Data Document (EIDD) is an established XML/NIEM model; later NENA i3 material also defines a JSON-based Emergency Incident Data Object (EIDO).
  • Use common status semantics. Normalize assigned, acknowledged, en route, arrived, cancelled, and cleared across vendors.
  • Prefer event delivery over repeated polling. Signed webhooks or managed message queues reduce delay and provide auditable delivery.
Location policy: Show the best authorized fix, its source, its age, and its uncertainty. When fixes disagree, do not silently blend them; elevate the discrepancy to dispatch.

UNITED RESPONDER PROXIMITY SYSTEMSECURITY AND GOVERNANCE
Non-negotiable controls

Responder location stays inside the agency

The system processes sensitive victim and fleet location. Access must follow the assignment, not curiosity, proximity, or possession of a link.

Identity and access

  • Agency single sign-on and multifactor authentication
  • Managed, registered vehicle devices
  • Role- and assignment-based access
  • Short-lived tokens; automatic revocation
  • No consumer access to vehicle coordinates

Data protection

  • Encryption in transit and at rest
  • Per-agency tenant separation
  • No sensitive data in URLs or push previews
  • Minimal local cache with remote wipe
  • Configurable retention and legal hold

Operational resilience

  • CAD remains authoritative
  • Visible stale-data and outage states
  • Last-known fix never shown as current
  • Redundant transport where the agency permits
  • Graceful fall back to radio/CAD workflow

Oversight

  • Immutable access and change logs
  • Supervisor review and export
  • Incident-bound analytics, de-identified by default
  • Security testing before production
  • Breach, outage, and misuse response playbooks

UNITED RESPONDER PROXIMITY SYSTEMPILOT PLAN
Phased delivery

Prove the workflow before anyone relies on it

Phase 0 - discovery

Confirm the doors

Document one agency's CAD, AVL, MDT, mapping, identity, network, records, and operating procedures. Agree who owns every interface.

Exit: signed architecture and data-flow decision.

Phase 1 - sandbox

Build with synthetic incidents

Connect a UNITED test event to a non-production broker and vehicle emulator. Implement identity, event state, route/ETA, freshness, and audit.

Exit: replayable end-to-end tests.

Phase 2 - shadow mode

Observe, do not guide

Mirror selected training or approved incidents. Compare the proposed view with CAD and radio outcomes; field users do not depend on it.

Exit: agency-approved safety and accuracy report.

Phase 3 - controlled operations

Limit scope tightly

Enable a small trained cohort, one incident class, and defined hours with dispatch oversight and an immediate kill switch.

Exit: formal operational acceptance.

  • End-to-end alert latency distribution
  • Victim and vehicle fix age
  • Route/ETA calculation success
  • Availability and recovery time

  • Driver-distraction observations

UNITED RESPONDER PROXIMITY SYSTEMRAPIDSOS DISCUSSION
Proposed partnership conversation

Ask for the integration path, not fleet access

UNITED is seeking a route to deliver trusted incident-side data into public-safety workflows and pair it, under agency control, with the assigned unit's existing AVL data.

One-sentence proposal

Partner with UNITED, one emergency communications center, and one CAD/field-responder vendor to test an assignment-scoped in-vehicle view of victim proximity, road distance, ETA, accuracy, and data freshness.

Requested from RapidSOS

  1. Technical discovery: identify the correct product and partner pathway for UNITED-generated emergency data.
  2. Sandbox: provide non-production documentation, credentials, sample payloads, and test procedures under RapidSOS terms.
  3. Integration guidance: confirm how incident IDs, location updates, cancellations, and field-responder delivery should be represented.
  4. Ecosystem introduction: identify a willing CAD/MDT partner and candidate public-safety agency for a limited pilot.
  5. Certification path: define security, reliability, support, and validation gates for any “RapidSOS Ready” or partner designation.

Questions for the meeting

  • Which RapidSOS product receives an app-originated emergency event with updated location?
  • Can that event be linked to a CAD incident and exposed to an assigned field unit through an existing integration?
  • Which party should calculate road distance and ETA: CAD, field app, mapping partner, or UNITED?
  • What data may be retained, and what must be deleted when the incident closes?
  • What sandbox, test PSAP/ECC, and certification resources are available?
  • Which security, uptime, support, and incident-response commitments are required?
Not requested: a public API showing responder locations; permission for UNITED to choose or dispatch units; production access before agency approval; or replacement of CAD, radio, 9-1-1 call handling, or responder navigation.

UNITED RESPONDER PROXIMITY SYSTEMNEXT ACTIONS
What to prepare

A credible pilot starts with six artifacts

1

Product demonstration

Show UNITED creating a test incident, including the “Stay Calm” trigger path, confirmation gate, location, accuracy, timestamp, and cancellation behavior.

2

Data dictionary

List every field UNITED can send, where it comes from, how often it updates, and how missing or stale values are represented.

3

Threat model

Document spoofing, replay, account takeover, unauthorized tracking, wrong-incident linking, device loss, and denial-of-service controls.

4

Integration adapter

Build the neutral incident model and a sandbox adapter. Keep CAD- and RapidSOS-specific code behind defined interfaces.

5

Vehicle prototype

Deliver a managed-tablet prototype that consumes only synthetic or agency-approved test data and demonstrates stale-data behavior.

6

Pilot packet

Include the concept of operations, architecture, test plan, privacy assessment, training outline, support plan, and stop conditions.

Sequence: demonstrate the alert and data first; agree the partner pathway second; build the field prototype only after the agency and CAD/MDT interfaces are known.
